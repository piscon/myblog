## I. It just wouldn't close

I bought this umbrella last autumn—light gray, eight ribs. I had used it for less than a month when, upon closing it, the button would make a "click" sound, but the shaft would get stuck halfway, refusing to press down no matter what I did. After that, every time I went out, I had to **carry it **into the subway, like I was holding a rather undignified flagpole.

Here is a list of the methods I tried, in order:

1. Dripping sewing machine oil into the button slot
2. Picking at the small holes in the shaft with a toothpick
3. Using a hairdryer on the shaft for three minutes
4. ~~Thinking about just throwing it away ~~(but I couldn't bring myself to do it)

> The first three didn't work. The fourth came closest to success, but every time I reached the trash can, I would turn back.

## II. After taking it apart

Having nothing to do on Saturday afternoon, I found a small screwdriver and pried open the plastic cap at the end of the umbrella handle. Out fell **a tiny grain of sand **—grayish-white, even smaller than a sesame seed.

*I didn't throw that grain of sand away; I put it in a tiny glass capsule bottle on my windowsill.* Now, it looks like a souvenir.

During these six months, I had woven several stories for this umbrella: a worn-out spring, a deformed frame, a defective product. Each one was quite plausible, and yet **each one was wrong **.

## Four: Fixing other things along the way

After that day, it was like a switch had been flipped. I dug out several things around the house that were "probably broken":

- The desk lamp: The bulb was just loose; a quick twist was all it needed.
- The drawer: A coin was stuck in the track; it wouldn't slide not because the wood had swollen.
- That old computer that never connected to the printer: The network cable was simply plugged into the wrong port.

**Not a single thing was actually broken. ** This conclusion made me feel a little embarrassed, yet a little happy.

## Five: Keeping that grain of sand

The umbrella is folded neatly now. The light gray fabric has a few old creases—they can't be washed out, and there's no need to.

A friend once saw the grain of sand in the glass jar and asked about it. I told her, "It's six months of subway rides." She didn't press further; she just said, "That's worth it."

> Some things aren't fixed; they are simply **finally looked at **.

It isn't raining outside right now. The umbrella leans against the door, folded short. Every now and then, I still press that button just to hear the "click"—purely to confirm that it's still there.
