## I. The phrase more accurate than an app

The weather app on my phone said it would be sunny all week, but on Saturday morning, the lady selling tofu said while handing me my change, "Bring an umbrella today; it'll rain this afternoon." When I asked how she knew, she pointed to her knee, then pointed to the pile of slightly wilted celery on her stall.

> "The vegetables know before the people do," she said without looking up, already tearing open a plastic bag.

As it turned out, it really did rain at three in the afternoon—not heavy, but enough to make those without umbrellas jog all the way home. Standing under the eaves of a convenience store, I thought back to that celery from this morning—**it really did know before my phone **.

## III. A false alarm

Of course, there are times when they are wrong. Last Wednesday, the flower vendor said, "It'll be windy tomorrow." I specifically moved the pothos plant from my windowsill into the house, but the next day there wasn't a breath of wind, and the sun was excessively bright.

When I went to ask her the next day, she gave a little laugh: "**When the flowers say there's wind, it just means they want to feel the breeze. **" I saved that sentence in my phone's notes; I'm more reluctant to delete it than any push notification.

*Later, I put that pothos back on the windowsill.* It probably doesn't mind being moved one extra time.

## IV. Why not just look at your phone?

Thinking about it, it's probably not a question of accuracy. A phone gives you "30% chance of precipitation," while the wet market gives you "bring an umbrella"—the latter involves a specific person who has already made half the decision for you.

> The former is data; the latter is a greeting. Sometimes, people need to be greeted.

The clerk at the convenience store downstairs is the same way. The day I bought an umbrella, she didn't say "it's raining," she just said, "Taking the long way around today?" and handed me the sturdiest one by the door. There was a handwritten label stuck to the handle: **"Don't lend it to others, or it'll get lost." ** The handwriting was crooked, but the word "lost" was traced twice.

## V. Closing

It feels a bit muggy outside the window tonight. I didn't open the weather app; instead, I went to the balcony to check on the pothos—the leaves aren't drooping, so there shouldn't be rain for now.
Still, I left the umbrella by the door. Auntie Tofu's knees are probably more reliable than my phone, but ** bringing an extra umbrella didn't really require anyone's permission ** anyway.
