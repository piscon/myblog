## I. Deciding not to take the subway

When I walked out of the building after work, it wasn't quite dark yet; a thin layer of orange remained in the west. My phone showed the subway would get me home in 12 minutes, the bus in 25, and walking would take 52. I stared at that "52" for a while, and suddenly felt **that I didn't want to be brushed off by 12 minutes today**.

So, I stuffed my phone back into my pocket and started walking in the opposite direction.

> Looking back, there was no reason for this decision. It was just that the wind that day happened not to be cold, and my shoes happened to be comfortable for walking.

The route was roughly this: first, walk north along the river embankment, turn into the old street at the second bridgehead, and finally cut through a cluster of alleys that I wasn't even sure existed. The navigation gave up by the third minute; the little blue dot on the screen stopped stubbornly in place, as if waiting for me to turn back.

## II. The first shop: An old bookstore about to close

The plane trees in the old street had been pruned; the half closest to the road was bare, while the other half still held its leaves. Beneath the trees was an old bookstore; the rolling shutter was halfway down, but the lights were still on.

The owner was moving cardboard boxes from the entrance inside. Seeing me standing there, he said, "Feel free to look, you've got ten minutes."

The shop was narrower than I imagined, with books covering three walls, leaving only a gap wide enough for one person to squeeze through sideways. I pulled out a few books at random:

1. An atlas from the eighties, the cities on the cover only a third the size of what they are now
2. A short story collection missing its cover, with "To Little Zhou, Autumn 1993" written on the title page
3. A book on how to repair clocks, filled with dense illustrations; I flipped through it for a long time without understanding a single gear
4. ~~A self-help book that looked quite new~~ (I put it back)

*In the end, I bought nothing.* The owner didn't rush me; he just moved the cardboard boxes a bit further out to make a little more room for me.

It wasn't until I stepped out that I noticed a handwritten note taped to the shop door: **"Closed on Thursdays, don't make a wasted trip.** The handwriting was crooked, but the word "don't" was specifically traced twice to make it thicker. I memorized that sentence; it was easier than memorizing any book[^1].

## III. The second wrong turn: An alley the navigation doesn't recognize

According to my memory, I should have turned right at the bridgehead, but turning right led to a wall. Turning left was a very narrow ramp, and at the end of the ramp, the path split into two more forks—the navigation went completely silent then, leaving only a gray circle on the screen.

I chose the left one. After walking for about five minutes, I came face-to-face with an iron gate with a sign hanging on it that read "No Thoroughfare." So, I retraced my steps, tried the right side, but the end of the right side was a parking lot. It wasn't until the third attempt that I saw a small alley beside the parking lot, one without even a single streetlamp, and without even a sign at the entrance.

It's a bit embarrassing to say, but out of these four times, the most comfortable one was actually **the second one**. Something was planted on both sides of that ramp, and leaves had fallen all over the ground, making a soft crunching sound when stepped on. Besides the "No Thoroughfare" sign, someone had also drawn a wobbly smiley face on the iron gate in chalk. I stood facing that smiley face for half a minute before remembering I needed to head back.

> Taking the wrong path only ceases to be a waste of time when you aren't in a hurry.
## IV. What’s in the alley

The alley I actually found was shorter than I expected, ending after about two hundred steps. But there were a few things along the way that made me unconsciously slow down:

- **Three pots of pothos** on a windowsill, their leaves gleaming under the streetlights
- A stack of empty flowerpots of various sizes against the base of a wall, the top one cracked
- An old washing machine being used as a stool, with a half-full, uncapped bottle of water sitting on top
- The faint sound of a piano coming from behind a certain door, stopping halfway through and never resuming

Turning right at the end of the alley, I suddenly found myself on the street where I live. It was less than two hundred steps from start to finish, yet it felt like emerging from a different season. The convenience store downstairs was lit up; the clerk was arranging cardboard boxes for the next day by the entrance. Seeing me coming from that direction, she paused for a moment and said, "Taking this way today, huh?"

"Yeah, took a bit of a detour," I replied. She didn't ask anything else and lowered her head to continue arranging the boxes.

## V. After arriving home

The first thing I did upon entering was boil some water. While the kettle was whistling, I stood by the window and watched the lights flicker on, one by one, in the building across the street, as if someone were slowly turning them on in sequence.

It wasn't until I took off my shoes at the door that I noticed two sycamore leaves stuck to the soles—one whole, the other torn in half. I wasn't sure if they fell from the tree on the slope or if I had picked them up earlier on the river embankment. I tucked the whole one into the book by the door that I've never finished reading, and threw the broken one away. I never used to do things like this, but since moving here, I've developed several irrational little habits: keeping pretty scraps of paper, folding convenience store receipts neatly, and rotating the plants on my windowsill in a fixed direction. **Perhaps when people arrive in a new place, they first try to steady their lives through these meaningless little gestures. **

Only then did my phone vibrate—a notification from the navigation app: "Route has been recalculated for you." I looked at that line and smiled for a while—it had been planning all night, only for me to walk back on my own, taking a route even more circuitous than any it could have provided.

Spreading out the notes I took today, there wasn't actually anything important:

- 12 minutes by subway, 52 minutes walking. What was bought with the extra 40 minutes? **Nothing at all **
- Didn't buy any books at the old bookstore, only remembered a note saying "don't make a wasted trip"
- The navigation went silent three times, but actually got it right once
- That bottle of water on the washing machine, I wonder who left it there
- ~~I should take the subway next time ~~ Probably not.

> Some evenings don't require "arriving"; just "passing through" is enough. People who take the long way home aren't actually trying to arrive late; they just want to make the journey a little longer.

The water is boiling. I brewed a bag of tea a friend stuffed into my hands when I was moving; the handwritten note on the packaging is still there, saying "settle in before drinking." I've been here for almost a week now; today feels like I've pretty much settled in.

The tea is lighter than I expected; it only started to have some flavor by the second cup. It reminded me of that coverless short story collection in the old bookstore earlier today. On the flyleaf, it said, "To Xiao Zhou, Autumn 1993"—I wonder if Xiao Zhou ever went back to that shop, and I wonder why that book ended up being sold. **When a book passes from one person to another, what lies between them is often not money, but many idle evenings just like this one. **

That layer of orange outside the window vanished long ago, replaced by a very clean deep blue. The convenience store downstairs has turned off half its lights; the clerk has probably finally finished arranging the boxes. I flipped through that atlas I didn't buy in my mind once more—**the city in 1993 was only one-third the size it is now **, and nowadays, you have to take a wrong turn three times just to find an alleyway that's only two hundred steps long.

It's quite nice, actually.
