##  One. The rain started at midnight

On the third day after moving into the new house, I was woken up by the sound of rain at midnight. It wasn't a heavy, refreshing downpour, but a ** dense and hesitant ** rain, tapping on the air conditioner unit, one hit after another, like someone patiently knocking on the door.

I lay there for a while, remembering that there were still a bunch of boxes left to unpack during the day, so I got up and boiled some water. The kitchen light was brighter than the living room, making the scratch on the counter top especially clear. The landlord said the house was previously lived in by an old couple who had lived there for over ten years. I always feel that these kinds of traces are more like proof of a "home" than any renovation.

>  Later, when it was almost dawn, the rain stopped. A small pool of water had accumulated on the windowsill, reflecting the lights of the buildings across from us that were still on.

##  Two. Things found in the boxes

In the morning, I started unpacking. I had planned to do it by labels: kitchen, books, clothes, miscellaneous items. But the first box was already lost - it contained books I bought in college, and the one at the bottom had a date written on the first page.

1.  "City and Dogs," the spine was already broken and had been repaired twice with transparent tape
2.  A poetry collection given by someone, with an expired subway ticket tucked inside
3.  Thick class notes, with neat handwriting that makes me a bit ashamed now
4.  still has half a box of ** highlighters **, which can still write colors

我把它们摊了一地，坐在中间发了很久的呆。原本打算「只留有用的」，最后~~全部扔掉~~变成了全部留下。人在搬家的时侯最容易心软，因为每样东西都连着一段具体的时间，而时间是不好称重的。

##  III. An Afternoon Fighting with the Printer

The new house didn't have a printer, so I carried it from the old place. Saying "carried" is not accurate, actually I moved it bit by bit, taking two breaks at the staircase corner along the way.

The task for the afternoon was originally simple: connect to Wi-Fi and print a test page. Result:

-  manual is missing, only remember the model
-  the official website driver page was redesigned, it took ten minutes to find the download entrance
-  after installation, it prompted "device not found", I restarted it three times
-  during the third restart, the cat knocked off the power cable

The most frustrating thing was, when it finally turned on with a green light, I realized ** there was nothing but a piece of paper in the box **. On the way to the convenience store to buy paper, it started to rain again, not heavily, but enough to make me forget to take the umbrella with me.

| Time | What I thought I was doing | What I was actually doing  |
|---|---|---|
| 14:00 | Install drivers | Find manual  |
| 15:30 | Print test page | Restart router  |
| 17:00 | Done | Go downstairs to buy paper  |
| 17:40 | Done | Finally printed a useless paper full of stripes  |

Later, I put that useless paper in the poetry collection. It was also a kind of "test passed," though not very elegantly.

In the middle, something small happened. The store clerk at the convenience store gave me ten yuan extra as change. I realized it twenty meters away and went back to return it. She was surprised, smiled and said, "Thanks, today is the third person who did this." I don't know if this counts as the first welcome message from this city, but it definitely felt more genuine than any "Welcome to stay" slogan.

When I came back, one of the motion-sensor lights in the hallway was broken. I was holding the paper, stepping on slightly damp stairs, and suddenly remembered that the building I lived in as a child was also like this, where the lights always needed a kick to turn on. ** Some habits are remembered more firmly by the body than the mind **, and no matter how new the house is, they can't be changed.

##  Four, Evening

When it got dark, I finally sat by the window. The tea was given to me by a friend before moving, who said, "Drink it after you settle in," with a handwritten note on the package.

>  "Don't rush to put everything back in place, the house will slowly get to know you."

I don't really believe such words, but that evening I was actually somewhat convinced. The rain stopped, the lights of the opposite building lit up one by one, the printer quietly blinked green in the corner, like a tame little animal. The cat was on an unpacked box, swishing its tail.
- [x] Boil water
- [x] Opened three boxes
- [x] The printer is connected (barely)
- [ ] There are nine boxes left
- [ ] Take back the umbrella

I stopped writing the list halfway. Some weekends don't need to be completed, it's enough that ** something happened **.

I sent a photo of the windowsill to a friend, she replied: "It finally looks like a place you can stay." I thought for a moment, and replied with a "Hmm." In fact, there were still four boxes in the kitchen that hadn't been opened, and the wardrobe door didn't close properly, but at that moment I really didn't want to explain.

>  The house doesn't need to be cleaned right away, and people don't need to adapt to the new place quickly. Taking it slow on both sides makes things easier.

In the evening, I heated up the soup left over from yesterday, using a bowl that had never been opened and was temporarily found in a box. There was a very fine glaze crack on the bottom of the bowl, which had been there before moving, and I had never wanted to throw it away. The cat was sitting by my feet watching me drink the soup, probably waiting for me to spill it accidentally. ** It waited for a long time, but didn't get what it wanted. **

##  Five, A little daydream before bed

After lying down, I suddenly became alert. The new bed was next to the window, I could see a small part of the sky, the clouds were lit a very pale orange by the streetlights. I counted how many words I had said today: I said thank you twice to the clerk, sent a message to a friend, said about seven or eight meaningless words to the cat, and didn't say a single word to the printer - ** mainly because I held myself back **.

I used to think moving was just moving things from one place to another, but now I think it's more like spreading time out again. Those books, that bowl with a glaze crack, that half box of fluorescent pens that could still write, weren't kept because they were useful. They were just present during a certain period of time, and then they just stayed.

I remembered the expired subway ticket I came across during the day, the station name on it had already changed. I've never been to that station, and I don't remember who put the ticket in the poetry collection. But the piece of paper is still there, the handwriting is still there, even the shallow crease on the back is still there. This is probably the entire reason for "keeping" it.

The rain didn't come again. After the air conditioner's outdoor unit became quiet, there was a very light, almost inaudible hum in the room, I didn't know if it was the refrigerator or something else. I listened for a while, but didn't look for the source.

##  Six, A little useless summary

If I compressed this day into a few sentences, it would be something like:

-  Old books are heavier than expected, but more difficult to part with than expected
-  Always leave an afternoon for things that are supposed to take five minutes
-  Cats' interest in electronic devices always appears at the worst possible moment
- ~~ Go to bed early~~ Eventually I stayed up until one o'clock anyway

The rain has started again outside, lighter than last night. I placed the poetry book within reach, thinking maybe tomorrow I should start unpacking the kitchen boxes, or maybe not. Either way.
---

The first weekend in the new house has passed like that. Nothing big happened, but every little thing is memorable.
